## Difficulty

The solver has to rebuild the electrical model of a 2×25 kV autotransformer-fed railway from the tables in the input files. The instruction gives the element equations, including the 3×3 self and mutual impedance matrix of every section, but not the nodal system. The solver must assemble it, get the autotransformer stamp and polarities right, and notice that every reading is a magnitude, so a reading is a gain times $ |U a + s b| $ plus an offset. The source voltage $ U $ and the train-current scale $ s $ are unknown at each of the 8 operating points and are refitted inside every candidate.

The seven error classes leave overlapping signatures. A raised impedance multiplies only the self entry of one conductor, so through the mutual terms it also changes how the other two conductors drop voltage. Raised impedances and rail leakage move the same quantities with different weights, an autotransformer that is out redistributes the return current, and a voltage offset imitates a local impedance change. Sensors are sparse, the noise is large, and some readings are gross.

The main difficulty is the error count: 9 and 10 on the development lines, 16 to 18 on the 12 private lines (207 in all). At about ten errors a greedy forward selection finds the truth. At 16 to 18 in at most 23 sections a wrong early choice can only be undone by a joint move (splitting, merging, shifting or exchanging errors).

Uniqueness is relative to the stated ranges and count limits. Within them, removing a true error costs at least 80, a spurious addition gains less than 30, and the best single replacement is worse by at least 80 even with the fit boxes opened to twice the stated ranges (over the 14 lines: smallest rise 81.9, largest addition gain 16.1).

**Simple methods.** No solver was used to choose the lines (see below). Two baselines were run on the final data afterwards, as a measurement (`tests/provenance/evidence/baseline_results.txt`, sources beside it). Both use the exact coupled model. The greedy one adds the best single error and then prunes: 8 of the 12 private lines, one campaign of three. The beam one keeps several partial diagnoses and also tries swaps and pair additions: 9 lines, one campaign. The reference passes all 12 lines. A program must pass every line of every campaign, so both are rejected.

## Reference solution

`solution/solve.sh` installs `solution/solver.py` as `/root/results/solver.py` and runs it on the development campaign, so the diagnosis is computed from the inputs and not copied from the key. For each line it solves the complex nodal equations of the coupled three-conductor network as a low-rank (Woodbury) update of the nominal solve, fits the two nuisances of every operating point by Gauss-Newton with Huber weights inside each objective evaluation, and searches the hypotheses with exact evaluations and a Levenberg-Marquardt refit of all continuous values. Many candidate fits are solved as one batch. Candidates are ranked after projecting out the columns of the current hypotheses, so a new error is judged by what the present ones cannot explain. The search is a class-diverse forward beam of single additions, refined by pruning, same-class swaps, pair additions among nearby candidates, two-for-two exchanges, shifts along the line, merges and splits, until nothing improves. Readings beyond 6 standard deviations are the gross errors. Lines run on two worker processes, and every search budget is a count and not a time, so two runs write identical bytes. One run takes about 50 s on the development campaign and 370 s, 750 s and 600 s on the three private campaigns, on 2 CPUs. The same machine later measured twice as slow (100 s for the development campaign), so the 3000 s limit leaves a factor of 2 to 4.

The reference was developed against candidate lines and improved after it failed on some of them, which is why it ranks by projection and has split, merge and shift moves. The truth does not come from it. A separate generator (kept with the author) injects the configuration and the gross readings and draws every constant, length, substation, autotransformer position, train and sensor separately for each line.

## Choice of the lines

No solver was involved in choosing any line. The generator drew each line with a fixed seed and a fixed specification (number of nodes and number of errors of each class) and accepted it only by structural rules: the rules of the instruction, a cap on how many errors can share a zone, and every class present. The specifications asked for 16 to 18 errors, and every shipped line has 16 to 18. These rules were checked again afterwards on the shipped data by independent code (below). Four lines were redrawn after the checks, always because of a rule and never because of how a solver did on them:

- H1/L2 (rise 64.3 against the required 80 in the exhaustive audit): redrawn with seed 7201.
- Development campaign (L1 had a rise of 73.9): both lines redrawn with seed 810002 (L2 had passed).
- H3/L3 (the gross-reading bound failed after refitting: 7.55 against 8): the refit bound was added to the generator and the line redrawn. Seeds 7301 to 7305 gave lines with only 14 or 15 errors, so a minimum of 16 errors was added to the generator; 7306 was stopped after 60 minutes without an accepted line; 7307 was accepted.

The instruction therefore says nothing about selection, because there was none beyond these rule-based redraws.

## Contested conventions

- **Coupled conductors, constant matrix.** The three conductors of a section are coupled by a symmetric impedance matrix, the same for every section and every operating point, with a common mutual resistance $ r_e $ and three mutual reactances, one per pair. The values follow Carson's earth-return formulas at 50 Hz (Carson 1926; Mariscotti and Pozzobon 2003): $ r_e = \pi^2 f \cdot 10^{-4} = 0.049\ \Omega/\mathrm{km} $, the generator draws 0.045 to 0.055, and $ x_{ij} = 0.1445 \log_{10}(D_e / D_{ij}) $ with $ D_e = 658 \sqrt{\rho / f} $ m. With spacings of 3.5 m (contact wire and feeder), 5.5 m (contact wire and rail) and 8.5 m (rail and feeder) and soil resistivities from 30 to 1000 $ \Omega $m this gives 0.31 to 0.42, 0.28 to 0.39 and 0.26 to 0.37 $ \Omega/\mathrm{km} $; the drawn ranges are 0.32 to 0.44, 0.30 to 0.40 and 0.27 to 0.37. The self resistance contains $ r_e $, and the self reactance contains the mean of its two mutual reactances, so the matrices are positive definite (checked at generation). A real line has different geometry in different sections; the task fixes one matrix per line. An error multiplies the self impedance of one conductor of one section and leaves the mutual terms, which is a modelling choice.
- **Illustrative parameters.** The line constants, substation windings and error ranges are typical orders of magnitude for a 2×25 kV line (Hill 1994; Kiessling et al.), not measurements of one named line. I have no citation for the exact ranges, so the task rests on the self-contained model in the instruction and does not claim to reproduce a particular railway.
- **Constant-current trains** instead of constant power. A constant-power train needs an inner iteration and its own metering assumption. Constant current keeps each operating point linear in $ U $ and $ s $.
- **Magnitude-only readings** instead of synchronised phasors, because field rms meters do not give phase.
- **Unknown $ U $ and $ s $ per operating point.** With them known the problem is much easier and unlike a real feed, where the source voltage floats with load and supply (EN 50163 defines the permitted band; the range used here is a modelling choice) and the train current is only metered roughly.
- **Autotransformer** as a centre-tapped element with a leakage impedance, magnetising branch neglected.
- **Evidence thresholds 80 and 30.** The cost is close to a chi-square, so a pure-noise parameter gains a few units. A search over hundreds of candidates inflates that, and the largest gain found on the shipped lines is 16.1, so 30 separates noise from structure and 80 leaves a margin for real errors.

## Verification

The verifier runs in a separate no-network environment with three private campaigns of four lines each and the two-line development campaign, with their truth, as checksummed archives unpacked at test time. The development campaign is included, staged exactly as the public directory (its key file too), so the stated requirements are tested on every campaign named. The isolation pattern is adapted from the `navigation-sensor-calibration` task in this repository (Apache License 2.0). The solver runs twice per campaign with the instructed command line, 3000 s wall-clock limit, 2 CPUs, 3 GiB of memory in total for the program and everything it starts (summed proportional set size), at most 64 processes and threads, files of at most 16 MiB, and the output directory as the only writable location apart from `/dev/shm`. The two reports must be byte-identical. After the solver and its processes exit, any file left in `/dev/shm` fails the run. The solver account's System V shared-memory segments, semaphores and message queues are removed before and after every run, and POSIX message queues are disabled, so no state carries over. Ten tests per campaign check the schema, each result field and repeatability.

The 12 private lines have 207 errors (34 contact-wire, 24 feeder, 38 rail, 36 leakage, 20 autotransformer, 31 offset, 24 gain) and 64 gross readings, and every line has every class. The reference reproduces all of them, and the isolated verifier returns reward 1 on it (40 tests, 8 solver runs in 7122 s in all, on the slower measurement of the machine).

**Tolerances.** The tolerances are 0.2, 0.25, 0.4, 1.0, 20 V and 4 percentage points. They are a resolution window, not a confidence interval. The standard error of every size at the truth is at most a quarter of its tolerance (largest 0.239 over the 14 lines), so the window is at least four standard errors wide. Values inside it that lie several standard errors from the truth are not statistically consistent with the readings, and I accept that. A tighter window would fail an exact estimator. A robust fit started half a tolerance away from the truth, with the true support kept, lands at most 0.69 of a tolerance away on the 204 continuous values of the 14 lines, with a median of 0.08, and two values lie beyond 0.5 (largest: 3.52 standard errors). The keys, the autotransformer list and the gross readings must be exact.

**Gross readings.** At the true sizes every gross reading has a residual of at least 8.86 and every other reading at most 3.69 (limits stated: 8 and 4.5). With the sizes refitted by minimising the stated cost and every reading kept, the smallest gross residual over the 14 lines is 8.76 and the largest other residual is 3.67, so the stated bounds hold in both cases; the reference flags beyond 6.

**Checks.** A second implementation written from the instruction text alone, not from the generator (`cleanroom_check`: it reproduces the noise-free readings of 12 random lines of 12 to 24 nodes, every error class active, to 2.6e-10), and a dense assembly of the whole network without the low-rank update agree with the generator and the reference to rounding. The post-hoc rule audit re-evaluates the rules of the instruction on every line independently of the generation run and passes on all 14 lines (smallest evidence 95.6 against 80). A separate exhaustive audit tries every other location and class for each true error with the ranges opened to twice their width: smallest rise 81.9, largest addition gain 16.1, and no series-equivalent placement of an error.

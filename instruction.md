# Autotransformer Railway Diagnosis

A 2×25 kV railway line is fed through autotransformers. The stored model of the line assumes the design impedance for every section of the contact wire, the negative feeder and the rail, every autotransformer in service, voltage sensors that are calibrated and current meters that are exact. The real line can differ from that model, and any single reading can be a gross error. Write `/root/results/solver.py`, a reusable program that takes the rms values measured at the operating points of a line and reports which sections have an increased impedance or an increased rail leakage, which autotransformers are out of service, which voltage sensors carry an offset, which current meters have a gain error, and which readings are gross errors.

## Inputs

`/root/data` holds the development campaign, lines L1 and L2. Every campaign directory contains the following files; the paths below are those of the development campaign:

- `/root/data/campaign.json`, listing the line IDs under the key `lines`.
- `/root/data/line_<ID>.json` for each listed ID, with `constants`, `lengths`, `substations`, `autotransformers`, `operating_points` and `sensors`. The nodes of the line are numbered from 0 along the line, and section $ j $ joins node $ j $ and node $ j+1 $; entry $ j $ of `lengths` is the length of section $ j $ in kilometres, and the contact wire, the negative feeder and the rail each run along every section. `constants` gives the self resistance and reactance per kilometre in $ \Omega/\mathrm{km} $ of the contact wire (`r_contact`, `x_contact`), of the negative feeder (`r_feeder`, `x_feeder`) and of the rail (`r_rail`, `x_rail`), the mutual resistance `r_mutual` common to every pair of conductors and the mutual reactances in $ \Omega/\mathrm{km} $ between the contact wire and the rail (`x_mutual_cr`), between the contact wire and the negative feeder (`x_mutual_cf`) and between the rail and the negative feeder (`x_mutual_rf`), the leakage conductance `g_leak` of the rail to earth in siemens per kilometre, the earthing resistance `r_ground` in ohms of the rail at every substation, and the leakage impedance `r_at` $ + j $ `x_at` in ohms of an autotransformer. Each entry of `substations` has a `node` and the resistance `r` and reactance `x` in ohms of each of its two windings. `autotransformers` lists the numbers of the nodes at which an autotransformer is installed; none of them is a substation node. Each operating point has `trains`, whose entries have a `node`, the rms `current` in amperes and the lagging phase angle `angle_deg` in degrees. A sensor has `index`, `type`, `sigma` and, according to its type, a `node` or a `section`; the sensors are numbered from 0 in the order of the list, so `index` is the position of the sensor in `sensors`; its reading is an rms magnitude with the unit of its type:
  - a `contact_voltage` sensor at a node reads the voltage between the contact wire and the rail of the node, in volts, and a `feeder_voltage` sensor at a node the voltage between the negative feeder and the rail of the node;
  - a `rail_voltage` sensor at a node reads the voltage between the rail of the node and earth, in volts;
  - a `substation_current` sensor at a substation node reads the current that the first winding of the substation delivers to the contact wire, in amperes;
  - a `contact_current`, `feeder_current` or `rail_current` sensor in a section reads the current of that conductor in the section, from node $ j $ to node $ j+1 $, in amperes.
- `/root/data/readings_<ID>.npz` for each listed ID, whose array `z` has one row per operating point, in the order of `operating_points`, and one column per sensor, in the order of `sensors`, so the column of the sensor with the index $ i $ is $ i $.

The development campaign directory also holds `/root/data/development_key.json`, which gives the true diagnosis of L1 and L2 in the format of the required result below; the other campaigns have no such file.

Analyse the line in the single-phase equivalent: steady state at one frequency, rms phasors, voltages relative to earth, and the reactance of an inductive impedance positive. At every node the line has a contact wire $ C $, a rail $ R $ and a negative feeder $ F $, with the voltages $ V_C $, $ V_R $ and $ V_F $. In every section the three conductors are coupled. Let $ \ell $ be the length of the section and $ Z $ the symmetric $ 3 \times 3 $ matrix, in $ \Omega/\mathrm{km} $, whose rows and columns are in the order contact wire, rail, negative feeder, with the diagonal entries $ z_C = $ `r_contact` $ + j $ `x_contact`, $ z_R = $ `r_rail` $ + j $ `x_rail` and $ z_F = $ `r_feeder` $ + j $ `x_feeder`, and the off-diagonal entries $ z_{CR} = $ `r_mutual` $ + j $ `x_mutual_cr`, $ z_{CF} = $ `r_mutual` $ + j $ `x_mutual_cf` and $ z_{RF} = $ `r_mutual` $ + j $ `x_mutual_rf`. The vector of the voltage differences of the contact wire, the rail and the negative feeder between node $ j $ and node $ j+1 $ (the voltage at node $ j $ minus the voltage at node $ j+1 $) is $ \ell Z $ times the vector of the currents of the three conductors in the section, from node $ j $ to node $ j+1 $. The contact wire and the negative feeder have no shunt admittance. The rail has the leakage conductance `g_leak` times the length of the section, shared equally between the rail at the two ends of the section, as a conductance from the rail to earth (in a section with increased rail leakage this conductance is $ 1+k $ times larger), and at every substation node the rail is also connected to earth through the resistance `r_ground`. Each substation has two windings with the internal impedance $ z_s = $ `r` $ + j $ `x` each. The first winding has its negative terminal on the rail and its positive terminal on the contact wire of the substation node, and the second has its negative terminal on the negative feeder and its positive terminal on the rail of that node. Each winding is a voltage source of the phasor $ U $ in series with $ z_s $, so the first winding delivers the current $ (U - (V_C - V_R)) / z_s $ to the contact wire and takes it from the rail, and the second delivers $ (U - (V_R - V_F)) / z_s $ to the rail and takes it from the negative feeder. The voltage $ U $ is a positive real number, the same for both windings of every substation in an operating point, it is the reference of all phase angles, and it is unknown in every operating point, where it lies between 24.5 kV and 30 kV. An autotransformer at a node is a three-terminal element between the contact wire, the negative feeder and the rail of that node, with $ z_{at} = $ `r_at` $ + j $ `x_at`: the current $ I = (V_C + V_F - 2 V_R) / (2 z_{at}) $ flows from the contact wire into the element, the same current $ I $ flows from the negative feeder into the element, and the current $ 2I $ flows from the element into the rail. A train is a constant current source: with `current` $ = I_t $ and `angle_deg` $ = \varphi $ it takes the current $ I_t e^{-j\varphi} $ from the contact wire of its node and returns it to the rail of the same node, except that the train metering is not exact: in every operating point the true currents of all its trains are the listed `current` values multiplied by one common factor $ s $, unknown, a positive real number between 0.92 and 1.08 that differs from one operating point to another, the angles being exact. A reading is the magnitude of the quantity it measures plus independent Gaussian noise with standard deviation `sigma`.

In the actual line some sections of the contact wire can have a self impedance, resistance and reactance together, that is $ 1+a $ times the nominal one, with $ a $ a real number between 0.2 and 0.8; some sections of the negative feeder can have a self impedance $ 1+a $ times the nominal one, with $ a $ between 0.25 and 1.0; some sections of the rail can have a self impedance $ 1+a $ times the nominal one, with $ a $ between 0.4 and 1.6 (an increase multiplies the diagonal entry of that conductor in $ Z $ for that section and leaves the mutual impedances unchanged); and some sections can have a rail leakage conductance $ 1+k $ times the nominal one, with $ k $ between 1 and 4. Some autotransformers can be out of service, which removes the element from the line. Some `contact_voltage` and `feeder_voltage` sensors can read the true magnitude plus an offset of 20 to 80 volts in magnitude and of either sign, the same in every operating point; the `rail_voltage` sensors have no offset. Some current meters, of any of the four current types, can read $ (1+g) $ times the true magnitude, with $ g $ of magnitude 0.04 to 0.12 and of either sign, the same in every operating point. Only the locations listed here can be in error, and each location (a conductor in a section, the leakage of a section, an autotransformer or a sensor) has at most one error. In any operating point any reading can carry a gross error, displaced from the value the sensor would otherwise read by 10 to 20 standard deviations, independently of the other operating points. Every range given as 'between' or 'to' includes its limits.

## What counts as an error

Define the cost of a configuration as $ \sum c(\rho) $ over all readings, where $ \rho $ is the reading minus the model reading, divided by `sigma`, and

$$
c(\rho) = \rho^2 \ \text{for}\ |\rho| \le 3, \qquad c(\rho) = 6|\rho| - 9 \ \text{otherwise,}
$$

minimised over the source voltages, the train-current factors $ s $ and the sizes of all errors in the configuration. Taking any one true error out of the true configuration raises the cost by at least 80. Adding any one further error to the true configuration, at any other location and of any class, with all sizes refitted, lowers the cost by less than 30. A candidate that gains less than 30 is therefore not an error and must not be reported.

Every line of every campaign, the development one included, has these further properties. Replacing any one true error by a single error at another location or of another class raises the cost by at least 80, and replacing it by two other errors raises it by at least 40. Replacing any two true errors that act between the same two substations by two other errors raises it by at least 80. The replacement by a single error was checked at every other location of every class, with all sizes refitted and the allowed ranges opened to twice their width; the other replacements were checked on the candidates that rank best in a screening of all locations and classes; an error of a sensor at a substation node counts with the stretch of line between that substation and the previous one (the one with the lower node number), and errors of sensors at the first node of the line were left out of the two-error check. Two adjacent sections of the contact wire that meet at a node with no train, no autotransformer in service, no substation and no `contact_voltage` sensor carry the same current, and so do two adjacent sections of the negative feeder that meet at a node with no autotransformer in service, no substation and no `feeder_voltage` sensor; an increase can be moved from one of two such sections to the other, scaled by the ratio of their lengths, without changing any reading. In every line no section with a contact-wire error ends at such a node of the contact wire, and no section with a feeder error ends at such a node of the feeder. At the true configuration the standard error of the estimate of every error size is at most a quarter of its tolerance (the tolerances are listed under Requirements). In that configuration, with $ \rho $ taken after the minimisation over the source voltages and the train-current factors, every gross reading has $ |\rho| \ge 8 $ and every other reading has $ |\rho| \le 4.5 $. The same two bounds hold when the sizes of the true errors are refitted by minimising the cost above with every reading kept.

## Other campaigns

The program must also work on three more campaigns that are not given here. Each has at most six lines of up to 24 nodes, with exactly three substations (one at each end of the line and one at an inner node), at most eleven autotransformers, up to 50 sensors, exactly 8 operating points with one to three trains each, and between 16 and 18 persistent errors per line, not counting gross readings (the development lines have 9 and 10). The program is started with

```text
python3 -B solver.py --input-dir CAMPAIGN_DIR --output-dir OUTPUT_DIR
```

These campaigns use the same file layout, sensor types, error types and conventions as the development campaign, but the lines are different: the number of nodes, the lengths, the line constants, the substations, the autotransformer positions, the trains, the sensor placements and the number and size of the errors all change.

Only `solver.py` is copied to the machine that runs it, so it must be self-contained. Campaign data are available only through `CAMPAIGN_DIR`, which the program must not modify. The program runs as an unprivileged user with `HOME` and `TMPDIR` set to `OUTPUT_DIR`. Every file it writes must go to `OUTPUT_DIR`, which exists, is empty and is writable; apart from `/dev/shm`, which is covered below, no other location is writable. The machine has no network access and offers Python with NumPy and SciPy and no other third-party package apart from pytest and pytest-json-ctrf. A `multiprocessing` worker pool is allowed, but once the program and the processes it started have exited, no file of the program's account may remain in `/dev/shm`; processes still running 20 seconds after the program exits are killed. Shared-memory objects (the files of the program's account in `/dev/shm`, and its System V shared-memory segments, semaphores and message queues) and any leftover process are removed before and after every invocation, so no state carries over between invocations; POSIX message queues are not available. Runs are deterministic: hash randomisation is disabled, the global `random` and `numpy.random` generators are seeded with 0, and numerical libraries use one thread.

Each run must finish within 3000 seconds of wall-clock time, counted from its start and not as CPU time, on 2 CPUs (`os.cpu_count()` may report more CPUs than that; `os.sched_getaffinity(0)` gives the 2 the program may use). The program and all the processes it starts may use at most 3 GiB of memory together (shared pages are counted once), and no single process may allocate more than 3 GiB of data memory (an allocation counts when it is made, whether or not its pages are used). At most 64 processes and threads may exist at once, and no written file may be larger than 16 MiB; standard output and standard error are each written to a file and each is held to the same limit.

## Required result

After writing the file `diagnosis.json` in `OUTPUT_DIR` the program exits with status 0; for the development campaign, run with `--output-dir /root/results/development`, this is `/root/results/development/diagnosis.json`. The file is standard JSON, a JSON object with the single key `lines`; no JSON object in the file repeats a key, and every key that names a section or a sensor is written in canonical decimal form, without sign, leading zeros, spaces or decimal point. The value of `lines` is a JSON object with exactly one key per line ID of the campaign, and each line entry is a JSON object with exactly these keys:

- `contact_wire_increases`, an object whose keys are the decimal numbers, counted from 0, of the sections whose contact wire has an increased impedance and whose values are the fractions $ a $, positive numbers, with no other keys.
- `feeder_increases`, the same for the negative feeder.
- `rail_increases`, the same for the rail.
- `rail_leakage_increases`, an object whose keys are the decimal numbers of the sections with an increased rail leakage and whose values are the fractions $ k $, positive numbers, with no other keys.
- `autotransformers_out`, the list of the numbers of the nodes whose autotransformer is out of service, integers in strictly increasing order.
- `voltage_sensor_offsets`, an object whose keys are the decimal sensor indices of the `contact_voltage` and `feeder_voltage` sensors with an offset and whose values are the offsets in volts, non-zero numbers such as $ -40 $, with no other keys.
- `current_meter_gains`, an object whose keys are the decimal sensor indices of the current meters with a gain error and whose values are the gains in percent, non-zero numbers such as $ -8 $ for $ g = -0.08 $, with no other keys.
- `bad_measurements`, the list of the pairs `[operating point, sensor index]` of integers, with operating points counted from 0, of the readings that carry a gross error, strictly increasing in lexicographic order.

## Requirements

A valid solution meets every requirement below for every line of every campaign the program runs on, and a program that misses any requirement for any line is not a valid solution:

- `bad_measurements` equals the true list exactly.
- `autotransformers_out` equals the true list exactly.
- The keys of `contact_wire_increases` are exactly the sections with an increased contact-wire impedance, and each reported fraction is within 0.2 of the true one.
- The keys of `feeder_increases` are exactly the sections with an increased feeder impedance, and each reported fraction is within 0.25 of the true one.
- The keys of `rail_increases` are exactly the sections with an increased rail impedance, and each reported fraction is within 0.4 of the true one.
- The keys of `rail_leakage_increases` are exactly the sections with an increased rail leakage, and each reported fraction is within 1.0 of the true one.
- The keys of `voltage_sensor_offsets` are exactly the sensors with an offset, and each reported offset is within 20 volts of the true one.
- The keys of `current_meter_gains` are exactly the meters with a gain error, and each reported gain is within 4 percentage points of the true one.
- Two runs of the program on the same campaign write byte-identical `diagnosis.json` files, although the two runs use different `CAMPAIGN_DIR` and `OUTPUT_DIR` paths.
- The file has exactly the structure described under Required result.

Each reported value must meet its limit after the difference from the true value is rounded to nine decimal places. The true values are those of the configuration and gross errors used to generate the readings of each line.
